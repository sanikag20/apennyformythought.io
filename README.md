# A Penny for My Thought? A Penny for Mine.

> *I spend a lot of my time teaching machines to find patterns in data.
> I also spend a lot of my time trying to find patterns in myself.*

## Hello, I'm Sanika.

I have always been a very literary person.

I love reading. I love writing. I love getting unreasonably attached to a sentence because I like the way it sounds. I love turning small, ordinary observations into something worth thinking about. Sometimes I write because I have something to say. Sometimes I write because I don't know what I think until I see the words in front of me.

For a long time, writing was simply something I did.

Then I started studying technology.

I found myself spending my days working with data, machine learning, algorithms, models, and systems. I learned how to take something messy and unstructured and find patterns inside it.

And eventually I started wondering:

**What if I did the same thing with my own writing?**

That question is what led to this project.

---

## Why I Started This Blog

**A Penny for My Thought? A Penny for Mine.** is my personal writing space.

It is where I put thoughts that would otherwise remain thoughts.

Some pieces are serious. Some are ridiculous. Some are nostalgic. Some are about things I probably spent far too much time thinking about.

They are not written to answer a particular question or fit into a particular category.

They are simply a record of what I was thinking about at a particular point in my life.

And that, eventually, started to feel interesting to me.

Because a collection of writing is more than a collection of essays.

**It is a record of a person changing.**

---

# Why Put a Writing Blog on GitHub?

The obvious answer is that GitHub is for code.

But I think that is precisely what makes this project interesting.

My academic and professional life is increasingly centered around data and technology. My personal life has always had this other side that is much harder to structure: reading, writing, observing, thinking, remembering.

I don't want to choose between those two sides.

I want to see what happens when they meet.

So this repository is not meant to pretend that my Wix website is a software application that I built from scratch.

The live publication lives on Wix.

This GitHub repository is something different.

It is the **technical layer around the writing**.

It is a place to document the archive, think about its structure, experiment with the data hidden inside it, and eventually build tools that allow me to look at my own writing in ways I could not before.

---

# The Writing Is the Dataset

This is the part of the project that excites me the most.

When I think about a dataset, I usually imagine rows and columns.

But my writing is data too.

Every essay contains information.

Not necessarily the kind of information that fits neatly into a spreadsheet, but information nonetheless.

There is:

* language
* vocabulary
* recurring ideas
* emotions
* references
* people
* places
* questions
* opinions
* uncertainty
* patterns in the way I describe things
* patterns in the things I keep coming back to

If I keep writing for another five, ten, or twenty years, I will eventually have something quite unusual:

**a dataset that is made up of my own thoughts across time.**

And unlike most datasets I will ever work with, I know the person behind every observation.

That makes this dataset deeply personal.

It also makes it fascinating.

---

# What Can I Learn From It?

I don't want to use technology to tell me what I *should* think.

I want to use it to help me notice what I already think.

There are questions I cannot easily answer by simply remembering.

### What do I write about most?

Maybe I think I write about relationships.

Maybe I actually write much more about uncertainty.

Maybe there is a theme that appears again and again that I have never consciously noticed.

Text analysis could help reveal those patterns.

---

### How have I changed?

If I compare something I wrote at 20 with something I write at 25, will the difference be obvious?

Will my vocabulary change?

Will the subjects I write about change?

Will certain emotions become less prominent?

Will the same questions keep appearing in different forms?

A longitudinal dataset could let me explore those questions.

---

### What keeps coming back?

Sometimes we think we have moved on from something only to realize that we keep writing about it.

A future model could identify recurring themes across years of writing and show connections between essays that I never consciously made.

An essay written three years apart from another might turn out to be remarkably similar in meaning.

That would be interesting to discover.

---

### What am I like when I write?

I could eventually analyze things like:

* vocabulary
* sentence length
* writing complexity
* recurring phrases
* emotional language
* sentiment
* topics
* semantic similarity
* changes in tone
* frequency of certain concepts

Not because a model can tell me who I am.

It cannot.

But because quantitative patterns can sometimes make qualitative patterns easier to see.

---

# From Search to Self-Reflection

One of the first experiments I would like to build is semantic search.

Imagine being able to ask my own writing archive:

> **"Show me something I wrote about feeling lost."**

And getting back an essay that never actually contains the word *lost*.

Or:

> **"What have I written about wanting to belong?"**

Or:

> **"Find something I wrote when I was optimistic."**

Or even:

> **"Show me how I used to think about failure."**

This is different from searching a blog.

It is closer to searching a memory.

And because the archive belongs to one person, the search results become a way of revisiting parts of myself that I might otherwise forget.

---

# What Could This Become?

I don't know yet.

That is part of why I am building it.

Some of the possibilities I want to explore include:

### 01. Semantic Search

Represent my writing using embeddings and search it based on meaning rather than keywords.

### 02. Personal Knowledge Graph

Build relationships between essays, people, places, ideas, books, experiences, and recurring themes.

### 03. Theme Evolution

Track how certain themes appear, disappear, and change over the years.

### 04. Writing Similarity

Find pieces written years apart that are surprisingly similar.

### 05. Personal Timeline

Create a visual representation of what I was writing about at different points in my life.

### 06. Topic Clustering

Allow machine learning models to discover groups of related writing without me manually assigning categories.

### 07. Language Evolution

Explore how my vocabulary, sentence structure, tone, and writing style change over time.

### 08. A Personal Retrieval System

Eventually, I would love to be able to ask my own archive questions about my past writing and have it retrieve relevant passages or essays.

Something like a tiny, private search engine for my own thoughts.

# The Long-Term Idea

The more I think about this project, the more I like the idea of treating writing as a form of longitudinal data.

Most datasets tell us about a population.

This one would tell me about **one person**.

Me.

And unlike a conventional machine learning dataset, the observations would keep arriving.

Every new essay would add another point in time.

Every year would add another layer.

The dataset would grow alongside me.

That means this could eventually become a strange combination of:

**personal archive + writing portfolio + NLP experiment + longitudinal self-study.**

I think there is something beautiful about that.

---

# But There Is a Limit

I don't want to reduce myself to a dataset.

A sentiment score cannot tell me how I actually felt.

A topic model cannot understand an entire memory.

An embedding cannot capture every reason I wrote a sentence.

And a machine learning model should not get the final word on something as subjective as a human life.

That is not the point.

The point is to create another lens.

Writing gives me one way of understanding myself.

Technology might give me another.

I want to see what happens when I put the two together.

# Why This Project Matters to Me

Most of my technical projects begin with a dataset and end with an answer.

It begins with a question:

> **What can I learn about myself if I treat my own thoughts as data?**

I don't expect a model to answer that question completely.

But perhaps it can show me patterns I missed.

Perhaps it can connect things I had forgotten.
