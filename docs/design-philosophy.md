# Design Philosophy

## Designing a Place for Thought

**A Penny for My Thought? A Penny for Mine.** began as a writing project, but the way writing is presented inevitably becomes part of the experience of reading it.

The design therefore follows a simple principle:

> The interface should create space for the writing rather than compete with it.

The publication is intentionally personal, quiet, and slightly imperfect. It is meant to feel closer to opening someone's notebook than navigating a conventional content platform.

---

## 1. Writing Comes First

The primary purpose of the site is to give ideas somewhere to exist.

The visual design therefore supports rather than dominates the content.

This means prioritizing:

* Readability
* Comfortable spacing
* Clear hierarchy
* Minimal visual distractions
* Easy movement between pieces
* A recognizable personality

The goal is not to make every page look technically impressive.

The goal is to make the reader want to stay.

---

## 2. Personality Over Uniformity

A personal writing archive does not need to behave like a corporate publication.

The titles themselves reflect different moods, references, and periods of thought:

* *Went back to my Ibiza.*
* *Not Like Other Girls.*
* *World's Smallest Violin*
* *Caught in a landslide*
* *Like Logs in a River*
* *It's Wednesday My Dudes*
* *Yours Truly, Debby Downer.*
* *Almost there ft. Inner Peace*
* *Lost.*

The variety is intentional.

The publication should feel coherent without making every piece feel identical.

---

## 3. Negative Space

Space is part of the design.

A reader should not feel as though they are being presented with information at maximum density. Empty space creates a pause between ideas and gives longer pieces room to breathe.

This is particularly important for personal writing, where the emotional rhythm of a paragraph can matter as much as the words themselves.

---

## 4. Navigation as a Reading Journey

Navigation should answer a simple question:

> Where do I go next?

A reader may arrive at one essay because of its title and discover another because of its subject, mood, or writing style.

The archive is therefore more than a chronological list.

Over time, it can become a map of recurring ideas.

---

## 5. The Tension Between Structure and Subjectivity

This project sits between two very different ways of organizing information.

As a data scientist, I am used to thinking about:

* schemas
* categories
* metadata
* relationships
* patterns
* search
* structure

Writing resists many of these ideas.

A thought does not always have a category.

An emotion does not always have a label.

A paragraph does not always belong to one topic.

That tension is part of what makes the project interesting.

---

## 6. Technology Should Remain Invisible When Possible

The live publication is hosted through Wix.

That is a deliberate choice.

The reader does not need to know how the page was generated in order to experience the writing.

This repository serves a different audience: it documents the thinking behind the publication and provides a place for future technical experimentation.

The two layers therefore have different jobs.

**The website is for reading.**

**The repository is for understanding, documenting, and experimenting.**

---

## 7. A Living Design

The design of the publication is not considered finished.

As the writing archive grows, the information architecture may evolve.

Possible future directions include:

* richer categorization
* thematic navigation
* improved search
* interactive visualizations
* semantic relationships between essays
* personalized discovery
* computational analysis of recurring themes

Any technical additions should preserve the original purpose of the project.

The technology should reveal something interesting about the writing, not turn the writing into an excuse to build technology.

---

## Final Principle

The project is ultimately built around a simple idea:

> Good technology creates possibilities. Good design knows when not to use them.

The writing remains the center of the publication.

Everything else exists to give it somewhere to live.
